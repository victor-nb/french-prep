# Tâche 3 — Step-by-step guide to a successful B2 performance

The complete playbook: what happens on exam day (the ~5 minutes themselves), and what "B2" actually requires. Based on [`structure-type.md`](structure-type.md), [`../../AMELIORATIONS.md`](../../AMELIORATIONS.md) and the corpus analysis in [`sujets-par-frequence.md`](sujets-par-frequence.md).

---

## The format (so you train for the right thing)

Tâche 3 = the examiner reads you a statement/question (e.g. *« Vivre dans un pays étranger est difficile. Êtes-vous d'accord ? »*). **No preparation time.** You speak **~4 min 30 continuously** — it is a **monologue** (the back-and-forth of Tâches 1 & 2 isn't part of it). An examiner may interrupt only if your discourse isn't clear.

**Score targets:** B2 = **10/20** on the global Expression Orale score (NCLC 7); **12/20** = NCLC 8; **14/20** crosses into C1/NCLC 9.

---

## Step by step during the task

### Step 0 — First 10–15 seconds (while you start talking, not before)

1. Pick the side that's **easiest to argue**, not your real opinion.
2. Mentally grab **2–3 arguments + 1 concrete example each**.
3. Launch a ready-made opener immediately — it buys your brain time:
   > « À mon avis, c'est un sujet très actuel / qui divise l'opinion publique. Personnellement, je pense que… »

### Step 1 — Introduction (~30 s)

Rephrase the topic in your own words + announce your position. Never repeat the prompt word-for-word.

Then **announce your plan** — a reliable, works-for-every-subject closer to the intro that scores under the Coherence criterion and buys thinking time:

> « **Pour justifier mon opinion, je vous donnerai quelques arguments, suivis de quelques exemples.** »
>
> *(futur simple: « donnerai » — careful not to write/say the conditional « donnerais »)*

Vary the wording occasionally (e.g. « Je vais vous présenter les avantages, puis les limites, et enfin donner ma conclusion ») so it doesn't sound recited.

### Step 2 — Argument 1 (~1 min)

Always the same 4-beat pattern: **idea → explanation → example → mini-conclusion**.

> « **Tout d'abord**, … **En effet**, … **Par exemple**, … C'est pourquoi… »

The example is what raises your score — take it from your life, your country, or society in general.

### Step 3 — Argument 2 (~1 min)

> « **Ensuite**, … **Cela permet de**… **C'est notamment le cas de**… »

If you still have time, add a third argument with « **Par ailleurs**… ».

### Step 4 — The concession (~45 s) — this is THE B2 move

Acknowledge the opposite view, then push back:

> « **Cependant**, certaines personnes pensent que… **Je comprends ce point de vue, mais** je crois que… »

or the stronger pivot:

> « **Certes**…, **il n'en reste pas moins que**… »

Visible nuance is the single clearest B2→C1 differentiator. **Never skip it.**

### Step 5 — Conclusion (~30 s)

> « **En conclusion**, je pense que…, **car**… »

Optional opening at the end: « On pourrait même se demander si… »

### Step 6 — If the examiner reacts

Tâche 3 is a monologue — plan to fill the full 4 min 30 yourself. There's no routine relance, **but an examiner may interrupt to ask a question if your discourse isn't clear** — treat that as a sign to be clearer, answer briefly, and resume. Don't count on interruptions to use up time.

## What the graders score (and your countermeasure for each)

| Criterion | What gets you B2 |
|---|---|
| Lexical range | Precise theme vocabulary, complex sentences (relatives, subordinates) — not *chose/faire/beaucoup* |
| Grammar | Reliable tenses and agreements; correct **subjonctif** after « bien que », « il faut que » |
| Fluency | Even tempo for 4½ min, no long silences — one short « euh » is fine, no « euh » chains |
| Coherence | Connectors beyond *et/mais*: *tout d'abord, en effet, cependant, en conclusion* |
| Interaction | Mostly judged on Tâches 1–2; in Tâche 3, just stay composed if the examiner reacts |
| Pronunciation | Intonation that supports meaning (secondary at B2) |

---

## The 7 day-of-exam reflexes

1. Pick the easiest side to defend.
2. One concrete example per argument, always.
3. Connect everything with connectors.
4. Slip in one concession — the B2 marker.
5. Two well-developed arguments beat four shallow ones.
6. **Speak until the end** — silence costs more than errors.
7. Accept mistakes; fluency and clarity outweigh grammatical perfection.

---

## How to train between now and the exam

1. **Memorize the express template** in [`structure-type.md`](structure-type.md) until it's automatic — it works for ~every topic.
2. **Cover the top 6 themes** (Immigration, Travail, Éducation, Santé, Médias, Technologie = ~78 % of all prompts) with 5–8 « → » collocations each from [`../vocabulaire/vocabulaire-par-theme.md`](../vocabulaire/vocabulaire-par-theme.md).
3. **Drill the most recurrent subjects** first — « Vivre dans un pays étranger est difficile » is the #1 prompt in the whole corpus.
4. **Practice the real format**: `/tcf-pratique examen` — random subject, zero prep, 4 min 30 timed, then feedback. Do 2–3 of these per week.
5. **Read the 5 model answers** in [`exemples.md`](exemples.md) aloud to internalize the rhythm — imitate, don't memorize.

> **The key insight:** since there's no prep time, the skill you're really training is **sustaining structured speech for 4½ minutes on autopilot**. The template + pre-loaded examples are what make that possible.
